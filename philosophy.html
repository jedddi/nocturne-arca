The Maison · Est. MMXXVI

What we leave out.

Nocturne is a small house of premium instruments. We do not chase the new — we wait for the inevitable. Each object is conceived in shadow, refined by hand, and released only when nothing more can be removed.

01 — Ethos

Restraint as the final material.

Most things are designed by addition: another feature, another finish, another reason to be noticed. We work the opposite way. An instrument leaves the atelier only once every part that could be removed has been — until what remains feels less like a product and more like a fact.

We measure days, not hours. A Nocturne piece is meant to outlast the season that produced it, and the one after. Permanence is not a feature we advertise; it is the discipline behind everything we make.

02 — Voice

"Conceived in shadow, refined by hand, released only when nothing more can be removed."

03 — The Atelier

A workshop in Le Locle.

Our atelier sits in Le Locle, in the Swiss Jura — a town that has kept time for two centuries. The bench is deliberately small. Fewer hands means fewer compromises, and every instrument passes through the same set of them before it is numbered.

Nothing is assembled to a quota. A piece is finished when the maker is unwilling to improve it further, and not a day sooner. That is why editions are limited — not as a marketing posture, but as a consequence of how the work is done.

Clients are welcome to visit by appointment. The concierge arranges the journey.

04 — The Process

Slow by design.

A Nocturne instrument begins as a drawing and a list of removals. We prototype in material, not in renders — because a thing only tells the truth once you can hold it. Each generation is tested against a single question: does this make the object quieter?

The result ships with a certificate from the atelier, the maker's mark, and an edition number. To understand the materials and origin behind each piece, read its provenance.
